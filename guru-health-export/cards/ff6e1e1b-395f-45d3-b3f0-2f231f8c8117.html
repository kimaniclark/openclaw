Total Silence: The quality of your sleep matters just as much as the amount you get. The slightest sounds can wake you up out of a deep sleep which has lasting effects, even if you wake up only for a few seconds. It takes time for your body to return to that phase of deep sleep that your body needs after a full day. Complete silence is scientifically proven to be the most beneficial for human beings and sleep. However, if you fall asleep easier with noise-masking, white noise or pink noise – that’s alright. It is pretty clear that these devices work the best when there’s lots of environmental noise (which obviously isn’t good in the first place).

Cool Temperature: Studies have found that the optimal room temperature for sleep is around 60 to 65F. Anything too far above or below this range will likely cause some difficulty sleeping. Your body goes through a process called thermal regulation every night at around 9:00 PM on average. It does this to lower your core body temperature to create the ideal environment for deep sleep — essentially, your body cools you off to sleep better.

After Sleep

Creating a habit when you wake up is just as important as improving your actual sleep. What is the first thing you do when you wake up? 99% of us look at our phones. It will literally change your life if you don’t look at your phone 30 minutes - 1 hour after waking up.

Step 1

Walking: Wake up and go for a walk. First thing every morning 365 days a year. Do not take your phone. Walk the same route everyday, 20-40 minutes. Be present in nature, observe life, let your mind naturally wonder.

Psychology/Physiology

  • Nasal breathing (stimulates rest/digest parasympathetic nervous system)

  • Posture (stand tall, open heart chakra - increases testosterone)

  • Feet placement (awareness - be intentional in every step)

  • Thinking time (subconscious planning the day/visualisation/rehearsal)

Step 2

JOURNAL: Journaling means writing down your thoughts and feelings to understand them more clearly. You’ll be amazed at how far your pen can go. If you struggle with stress, depression, or anxiety, keeping a journal can help you gain control of your emotions and improve your mental health.
